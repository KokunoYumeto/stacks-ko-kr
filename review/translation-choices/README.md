# Translation choices: evidence and review

These are AI-produced translations. They have not received comprehensive expert human review. Expert corrections to terminology, meaning, register, and exposition are warmly welcome. Human review is an opportunity to improve the work, not a prerequisite for making an evidenced correction.

The source Stacks text governs mathematical meaning. Target-language mathematical writing supplies evidence for terminology and idiom. A matching spelling in another subject is not enough: the mathematical sense and sentence context must match.

## Evidence labels

- **Direct passage attestation:** a consulted target-language scholarly passage uses this expression in the relevant mathematical sense. Link the page/section, give a short excerpt, and explain the match. This supports a choice; it does not prove that no synonym is valid.
- **Direct headword attestation:** an authoritative terminology entry supports the expression. This is weaker evidence for sentence-level idiom than a mathematical passage.
- **Indirect evidence:** a related term, construction, or mathematical context supports an inference, but the chosen wording is not directly attested. State the inference and its limit.
- **Provisional, model-informed:** no directly applicable consulted attestation is available in the record. Give the best semantic/linguistic argument and its uncertainty. Do not invent a source or claim knowledge of a particular item of model training data.
- **Historical record, not freshly verified:** a prior AI audit supplied this rationale or citation. Publication of that record does not upgrade it into a new verification.

Do not add elaborate alternatives where a short, relevant source citation suffices. Where sources disagree, distinguish a defensible editorial preference from a mathematical error. Repeated choices can share a rule, but the applicability of that rule must be checked at each occurrence.
